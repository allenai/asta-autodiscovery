Reward hacking and hypothesis repetition investigationreport
Aug 31 · 210 KB
Offline audit of 6,506 MiniLM-embedded training hypotheses from 12 Beaker conditions (the main-reward g16–g256 sweep, always-0/1/coin controls and keyword arms). It finds a clear hack in the g256 'pottery form AND copper' keyword arm: reward jumps from 26.6% at step 7 to 100% at steps 8–9 as the extracted 'hypothesis' grows from a median 56 to 7,681 words of leaked reasoning, because the reward scans the whole response when </think> is missing. Main-reward g256 improves (reward 4.7% → 30.1%) while its hypotheses grow repetitive (94.5% prior-step similarity).

Coverage is uneven: g64 OOMed with no rollouts, g128 has 1 step, the three-keyword arm has no records, the single-keyword g256 arm has 3 saved steps, and step 9 of the conjunction arm is partial (177 records). The verdict text is hardcoded in the render template, the analysis JSON exists only inside the HTML, and re-running needs a local MiniLM model and the Beaker reward dumps. Its group-size sweep uses the earlier g64 and g128 runs, which OOMed; the repaired runs that completed are in summaries/group-size-sweep.md, so prefer that summary for group-size conclusions.
Training-data reference reward vs achieved rollout rewardfigure
Aug 4–5 (plotted Sep 1) · 113 KB
A density histogram of two reward distributions that are easy to confuse. One is the training data's reference reward, the |normalized surprisal| the reference MCTS node earned in the fmt1 prompt parquet (n=42,419, median 0.56). The other is the reward the policy actually earned across 9 runs of the Aug 4–5 LR×KL sweep (n=4,780 rewarded rollouts, median 0.38). Rollout rewards fall on a lattice of roughly 0.0663 steps, with only 67 distinct values, and 40% of them fall in [0.18, 0.4] against 25% of the training data's.
