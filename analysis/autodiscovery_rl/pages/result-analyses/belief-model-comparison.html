Belief model comparison

Three runs, one archaeology prompt, one pinned harness, and only the belief model swapped. The result is not a ranking: two of the three judges never delivered a usable verdict, and the one that did is scored by a rule that can only pay out in one direction.

The comparison was genuinely controlled

Held constant across all three arms: the pinned harness (asta-autodiscovery main@ebf75d55), the slime revision, a single archaeology prompt, group size 256, 10 steps, GRPO at lr 5e-6 with KL 0.01, the binary reward rule, and the execution model — gpt-5-mini ran the experiments in every arm. Only --belief_model changed, with n_belief_samples 3 throughout. Step 0 confirms it from the other side: before any training signal exists, the three arms' hypothesis distributions are statistically indistinguishable on every measured dimension (min p = 0.115, max KS D = 0.090).

Two thirds of every arm dies before the judge sees it. The harness accounting is exact — failures plus judged equals experiments in all three arms — so 60–68% of experiments failed executor/reviewer checks in the two working arms. A 0.0 reward in the rollout dumps therefore means either "the judge was not convinced" or "there was nothing to judge", and nothing in the rollout record distinguishes them.

Three trajectories, three regimes

Why gemini returned nothing

403 · Agent Platform API not enabled

Every belief call in the gemini arm hit the same wall, retried three times, and gave up into an empty distribution — 2,700 403s and 900 exhausted retry chains in a 311 MB log that contains not one [belief] line.

The failure is downstream of execution, not upstream: the code executor reported success 2,650 times in this arm, and the experiments were still marked 100% failed, because the missing verdict poisons the whole record. gemini-3.8-flash has not been evaluated as a belief model. Nothing in this run licenses a conclusion about how strict or lenient it is.

The reward rule decides the ranking

Reward fires when the judge's belief moves by |Δ| ≥ 0.2. With three prior and six posterior draws over five categories, Δ can only land on a coarse lattice — and luna's mass piles up almost exactly on the bar: 45.9% of its judgements sit at Δ = 0.2143, 0.0143 above the threshold.

A two-percentage-point move in the bar reverses the ranking. Any statement of the form "judge X is more permissive" is really a statement about where 0.2 happened to fall relative to one lattice point.

The reward only pays for disappointment

The posterior mean is a smoothed average pooled over prior and posterior samples, which bounds how far belief can travel upward. In gpt-5-mini's modal configuration — a 3× maybe_true prior, covering 69% of its judgements — the largest attainable upward shift is 0.1875, strictly below the 0.20 bar. It is arithmetically impossible to be rewarded for a result that makes a hypothesis look more true.

luna's column looks symmetric only because its broken prior pins the baseline at 0.5, which leaves room to move in both directions. Fix the parser and its asymmetry returns.

What this does not establish

  • Any judge's quality. gemini produced zero verdicts; luna's priors parsed in 15% of judgements; gpt-5-mini is the only measured arm, and its measurement is one-sided.
  • That the gpt-5-mini climb is a judge effect. Two later runs with the same belief model on the newer harness show no learning at all (g64: 4.7% → 0.0%). Whatever produced 4.7% → 30.1% here, it is not simply "gpt-5-mini judges well".
  • Causal attribution for any trajectory. One run per arm; belief model, seed and calendar date are not separable. Arm A also differs from B and C in minRuntime, CPU count, a wandb patch, and the Vertex credential bootstrap.
  • Step-resolved judging. Belief records carry no run/step/rollout id, only log order — a reconstruction by ordering fails a permutation test (p = 0.163). Per-step judged rates are not quotable.
  • That gemini's policy provably froze. Drift is bounded, not zero: |d| ≤ 0.31 by bootstrap CI, below the d = 0.25 this design can resolve.

What to do next

  1. Instrument the belief line before re-running anything. Log run, step, rollout_id, the raw sample counts, and an explicit judge_status ∈ {ok, prior_empty, posterior_empty, api_error}. Every ambiguity on this page dissolves with two extra fields; without them no re-run is interpretable.
  2. Fix luna's prior parse, then gate the re-run on a smoke test — at least 95% of judgements returning 3 prior samples. Until that passes, its numbers mean nothing.
  3. Enable the Agent Platform API and re-run gemini as a 1-step smoke test — minutes, against the 14h31m the dead run burned — with a fail-fast: if nothing has been scored after 64 experiments, kill the job.
  4. Fix the reward's directional asymmetry — drop the prior/posterior pooling, or make the threshold symmetric against the attainable range, or raise n_belief_samples from 3 to ≥10 so the lattice spacing falls below the effect sizes you care about. This is probably worth more than the choice of judge.
  5. Attack the 60–68% executor failure rate. More rollouts reach the judge under gpt-5-mini (39.8%) than luna (32.3%, p = 2.3e-8) — a gap comparable in size to the judge effect being studied, and the single largest source of wasted compute in all three arms.
  6. Then re-run the three-arm comparison properly: same day, same group size, ≥3 seeds per arm, and pre-register the metric as accept rate among judged rollouts with an intact prior, reported next to coverage and next to a threshold sweep rather than a single point.
